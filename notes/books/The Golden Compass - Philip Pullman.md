*The Golden Compass* has been showing up in my (automated) recommendations for a long time, so I decided to finally read it. It's the first book of Philip Pullman's acclaimed *His Dark Materials* trilogy, published between 1995 and 2000.

The book follows 11-year-old Lyra, who is being raised by scholars in an Oxford college. In Lyra's world, every human has a dæmon - an animal-shaped manifestation of their soul that is always with them. This bond between human and dæmon is key to the book's central conflict. When Lyra and her dæmon Pan uncover an abduction conspiracy and learn the unsettling truth about her parents, they join up with Lyra's gyptian friends and travel into the Arctic to rescue her friend Roger and other abducted children. Later, Lyra unintentionally provides the means to open a bridge to another world, and the book ends with Lyra and Pan crossing into the other world.

From start to finish, this book is a real page-turner. The story progresses at a rapid pace, and there are twists and turns every few chapters. That makes it a very easy read, but at times I found the story lacked depth. The witches, the aeronaut, and the college staff all seem fascinating, but we learn relatively little about them. And though we discover more about the background and motivations of some of the main characters, they mostly remain the same throughout the book. With more character development and a stronger sense of the larger story, this would have rated higher. It's a fun, fast-paced story, though, so I am eager to see what the second book, *The Subtle Knife*, brings.
