This is the fourth and final book in C.I. Chevron's *Mutants and Modifieds* series that reimagines classic fairy tales in a steampunk setting. Like most of the books before it, *Pride and Pistons* is set in a late 19th-century London that is filled with mechanical inventions and airships, alongside genetically altered humans, such as vampires and werewolves.

This time around, the story is about wheelchair-bound Rosalind, a former royal who is now the commander of an organization that polices dangerous mutants and modifieds. Her former lover leads a rival organization that protects those same people, and the two get tangled up in a *Little Mermaid*-inspired plot with lots of fighting, death, and an evil brother who must be vanquished.

I liked the first book in this series, [*Metal and Bone*](/books/metal-and-bone:-a-steampunk-cinderella-story-mutants-and-modifieds-book-1---c.i.-chevron), for its world-building in the steampunk fantasy setting of Victorian-era London. The [second](/books/cogs-and-fur:-a-steampunk-beauty-and-the-beast-mutants-and-modifieds-book-2---c.i.-chevron) and [third](/books/tooth-and-nail---c.i.-chevron) books had more focus on the romantic aspects of the fairy tale they were modeled after, and I found the same to be the case in *Pride and Pistons*. While there are still some fun new ideas, it's quite clear that the series has run its course.
