Although I discovered Brandon Sanderson’s writing relatively late and still have plenty of his prolific output to explore, I try to limit myself to one of his books per year. That's because the books in *The Stormlight Archive* series are all very, *very* long and tend to disrupt my reading schedule. Wind and Truth clocks in at 1,330 pages, the longest in the series so far - and it marks the end of the first half of what's meant to be a 10-book series. 🤯

The Stormlight Archive series is set on storm-ravaged Roshar, where warriors, scholars, rulers, and magical Radiants try to unite humanity while confronting ancient enemies, political backstabbing, and personal trauma. Wind and Truth covers a span of 10 days during which Dalinar, leader of the human coalition, prepares for a decisive contest with the god Odium, while all of his allies wage battles of their own in different places and planes.

The build-up to the final confrontations is, long but very eventful, which feels like a recurring issue throughout this series. The writing is good and engaging; there's just so much of it! This could easily have been two decent-sized books, which feels like a common theme for the series too. I regularly wished that Sanderson had taken a similar approach to Awaken Online, with a main story arc and then side-quest books that further explore specific characters and their adventures. 

Wind and Truth is a solid entry in the series and a good capstone to the first main story arc, but I am also somewhat glad to be done with it. I might at some point explore some of Sanderson's other writing in the same Cosmere universe.
